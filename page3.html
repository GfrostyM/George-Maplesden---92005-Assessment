New Zealand's native birds

In New Zealand there is a wide variety of unique and diverse native birds, and they make up a large amount of our country's biodiversity. Many of these birds are endemic, which means that this is the only place in the world that they will be found. Although, most birds can fly, in NZ lots of the native birds are flightless. This is because New Zealand used to be predator free as we are so isolated as a country, so over the years the birds adapted to not need wings to fly. Some of our most iconic birds such as the famed Kiwi, as well as the takahe and kakapo are all flightless. Despite this we also have many native birds that do fly such as the kea, fantail, tui and much more.

Endangerment

Unfortunately for our bird friends here in NZ many of them are becoming endangered and some are even close to extinction. This is mostly due to the introduction of pests and mammals that are eating their eggs and hunting them for food. With alot of the brids not being able to fly to safety, these predators pose as a big threat.

What are the solutions?

A great way that we can protect the birds is to create protected areas of land/bush. This would mean that the species of bird in this area can live and survive while not being hunted and killed by the predators. In terms of what you or me might be able to do to help the biggest things we can do is donate to conservation charities, report sightings of native birds and try to manage the predators by setting up pest traps and putting bells on our cats to give the birds warning, in order to escape.
